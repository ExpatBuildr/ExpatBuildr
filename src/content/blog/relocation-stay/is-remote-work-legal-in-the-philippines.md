Yes, and since April 2025 there's a real, formal legal framework covering it, which is a genuinely different situation than it was a couple of years ago. Before Executive Order No. 86 created the Philippines Digital Nomad Visa, this was a functional gray area — plenty of people worked remotely on tourist status with no dedicated legal category covering it. That's no longer true, and understanding what actually changed matters if your information on this predates 2025.

Here's what's actually legal, what the DNV covers, and what tourist-visa remote work still risks.

## The Digital Nomad Visa: the real answer for most of this audience

Executive Order No. 86 established a formal visa category specifically for people earning income through digital means for clients or employers based outside the Philippines. To qualify, you need to demonstrate remote work for overseas clients or employers, hold a passport from a country with a reciprocal arrangement with the Philippines, carry valid health insurance covering your stay, and pass a clean background check.

What it explicitly does not authorize: taking on Philippine clients or Philippine employment. The database-monitored compliance system flags violations of this boundary, with consequences up to revocation and deportation for confirmed cases — this isn't a soft guideline, it's an enforced condition of the visa.

**One honest gap worth flagging:** the specific income threshold for this visa isn't fully finalized in public guidance as of this writing. Some sources estimate a rough benchmark around $2,000/month, drawing comparisons to similar regional programs, but the Philippine government hasn't published a confirmed, hard number. If you're applying, verify the current figure directly with the Department of Foreign Affairs rather than budgeting around an estimate.

## What working on a tourist visa actually risks

Before the DNV existed, and still true for anyone not on it, working remotely while on a standard tourist entry sits in a genuinely different category than Thailand's more contested gray zone (covered separately, since the two countries' legal frameworks work differently enough that they don't map onto each other well). The Philippines has historically had lighter, less codified enforcement against quiet remote work on tourist status compared to some neighboring countries, and BI's practical focus tends to be on overstay and visa compliance rather than auditing what foreigners are doing on their laptops.

That said, "historically lighter enforcement" is not the same as "confirmed legal," and it's a materially weaker position than simply qualifying for the visa built for exactly this situation. If you can document consistent foreign-sourced income and qualify for the DNV, there's limited upside to staying in the ambiguous tourist-visa category instead.

## What the tax side of this looks like

Foreign-sourced income for non-residents generally falls outside Philippine tax obligations, with most liability remaining tied to your home jurisdiction rather than triggering local Philippine filing requirements — this is genuinely one of the more straightforward parts of the Philippines' framework compared to some other Southeast Asian destinations. This doesn't change your US filing obligations, which continue regardless of where you live; that side is covered in [how taxes work when you earn US income while living abroad](/blog/relocation-stay/how-taxes-work-when-you-earn-us-income-while-living-abroad).

## What actually changes your risk profile

- **Keep income entirely foreign-sourced.** The moment Philippine clients or Philippine-based payment enters the picture, you've moved from a category the law clearly supports into one it explicitly prohibits.
- **Get the DNV if you qualify.** It exists specifically to remove the ambiguity that tourist-visa remote work still carries, even with historically light enforcement.
- **Document your income consistently**, since that documentation is exactly what a DNV application requires — building the habit now saves scrambling later.

## Where this gets specific to you

Whether your particular income structure documents cleanly for a DNV application, what to do if your income is genuinely still ramping up and doesn't yet meet a comfortable threshold, and how this interacts with a longer-term visa strategy — those depend on your actual situation.
