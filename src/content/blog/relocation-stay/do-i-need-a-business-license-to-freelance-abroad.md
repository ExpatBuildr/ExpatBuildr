Generally no, not for the version of freelancing most people in this audience are actually doing — foreign clients, foreign payment, no local business activity. But that "generally" is doing real work in that sentence, and the exceptions are specific enough that assuming you're covered without checking is a genuine risk, not a formality.

Here's the actual distinction both countries draw, and where it applies to you.

## The core question isn't "are you a business," it's "are you doing business locally"

Neither the Philippines nor Thailand requires a local business license for income you earn from clients outside the country, paid into accounts outside the country, for work that doesn't involve local marketing, local employees, or a local physical operation. That's the version of freelancing this pillar is written for — a remote consultant, writer, developer, or service provider whose entire client base and payment infrastructure sits outside the country they're living in.

What changes the answer: the moment any part of that picture becomes local. Serving local clients, receiving local payment, hiring local staff, renting commercial space, or advertising your services within the country all shift you from "someone living here who happens to earn foreign income" to "someone conducting business here" — and the second category has real registration requirements in both countries.

## Thailand specifically: a real, low threshold exists, but it's about local sales activity

Thailand does have a business registration requirement for anyone conducting sales activity above a fairly low local threshold — generally interpreted around ฿20/day or ฿500 total in local sales value, obtainable through a certificate at your local district office. But this specific rule is aimed at local commercial activity, not foreign-client freelance income. If you're not selling anything to anyone inside Thailand, this particular threshold isn't the relevant concern for you.

The more relevant Thailand-specific tension is the same one covered in [how expats make money in Thailand without a work permit](/blog/relocation-stay/how-expats-make-money-in-thailand-without-a-work-permit): the question isn't really about business licensing, it's about whether your remote work itself, physically performed while you're in Thailand, counts as "working" under Thai law regardless of who's paying you. A business license doesn't resolve that question — the DTV does, by explicitly authorizing the activity itself.

## Philippines: cleaner, especially post-Digital Nomad Visa

The Philippines' 2025 Digital Nomad Visa framework is explicit that the entire point of the visa is authorizing foreign-income remote work without requiring local business registration, provided you're not taking on Philippine clients or Philippine employment. If your freelance work is genuinely foreign-client-only, you're operating within a category the Philippine government has specifically built a legal pathway for, rather than one it's silent on.

## When you'd actually need to register something

Registration becomes relevant, in either country, once you cross from "individual earning foreign income" into a few specific situations:

- **You want to open a local business bank account under a business name**, rather than simply receiving foreign payments into a personal or foreign account
- **You're hiring local staff or contractors**, which triggers labor and tax registration requirements regardless of where your revenue comes from
- **You're renting commercial space** — an office, a studio, a storefront — as opposed to working from a co-working space or your residence
- **You start taking on local clients**, even occasionally, which shifts part of your income into locally-sourced territory with its own tax and registration implications

None of these apply to the straightforward "remote freelancer with foreign clients" case most readers of this article are actually in. But if your business is starting to grow into any of these categories, that's the point where the "do I need a license" question stops being generic and starts depending on what you're actually building.

## The tax question this doesn't answer

Not needing a local business license doesn't mean you're free of tax obligations, in either the country you're living in or your home country. Those are separate questions with separate answers — we cover the US tax side specifically in [how taxes work when you earn US income while living abroad](/blog/relocation-stay/how-taxes-work-when-you-earn-us-income-while-living-abroad).

## Where this gets specific to you

Whether your particular setup crosses into "local business activity" without you realizing it, whether it's worth registering something even when you're not strictly required to (some freelancers do it anyway for banking or tax-optimization reasons), and how licensing interacts with your specific visa status are all situation-dependent questions a general article can't fully resolve.
