Most comparisons of Philippine long-term visas fail at the first step: they compare the visas to each other instead of comparing them to *you*. SRRV, a work visa, and the 13A marriage visa aren't competing options where one is objectively better, they're three different eligibility doors, and you only actually qualify for the ones that match your age, income source, and relationship status. The real question isn't "which is best," it's "which ones am I even eligible for," and then, among those, which fits your timeline.

Here's the direct comparison, then how to actually use it. See every guide in the [Relocation & Stay Strategy hub](/blog/relocation-stay).

## The three paths, side by side

| | SRRV (Retirement Visa) | Work Visa (9(g) / AEP) | 13A (Marriage Visa) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Who qualifies** | Age 40+, with pension or deposit capital | Sponsored by a Philippine-registered employer | Legally married to a Filipino citizen |
| **Upfront cost** | $15,000–$50,000 refundable deposit (age/pension-dependent) + ~$1,500 application fee | Employer-dependent; AEP fees + visa processing, no personal deposit | ~₱25,000–₱40,000 government fees, no deposit |
| **Ongoing cost** | ~$360/year maintenance fee | Tied to continued employment | None once permanent |
| **Right to work** | No: SRRV does not grant work rights on its own | Yes: that's the entire basis of the visa | Yes, though an AEP may still be required depending on the job |
| **Duration** | Indefinite, renewable annually | Tied to your employment contract | Probationary 1 year, then permanent, no expiry |
| **What happens if the underlying condition ends** | Deposit is refundable if you cancel | Visa is void if you lose the sponsoring job | Status is not automatically voided by separation, but complications can arise |
| **Best fit** | Financially independent, 40+, not tied to a local employer | Working for a Philippine company that will sponsor you | Genuinely married to a Filipino citizen |

## Why the "which is best" framing is the wrong question

Notice that these three don't actually overlap much in who qualifies for them. A 34-year-old solo founder running a US-based consulting business isn't eligible for SRRV (too young unless earning $50K parked as deposit), doesn't have a Philippine employer to sponsor a work visa, and isn't married to a Filipino citizen. On paper, none of these three apply to him, which is exactly why the fourth option, the Digital Nomad Visa, exists, and why it's the one most people in this exact situation haven't heard of yet. We cover all four paths, including the DNV, in **[How to Stay in the Philippines Long Term, Legally](/blog/relocation-stay/how-to-stay-in-philippines-long-term-legally)**.

So before comparing cost or convenience, the actual filter is eligibility:

- **Do you have a pension, or $15K-$50K in liquid capital you're willing to park in a Philippine bank, and are you 40+?** SRRV is on the table.
- **Does a Philippine-registered company want to formally employ and sponsor you?** Work visa is on the table.
- **Are you legally, genuinely married to a Filipino citizen?** 13A is on the table.
- **None of the above, but you have steady foreign-sourced income?** You're looking at the Digital Nomad Visa or tourist extensions, not these three.

Most people asking "which visa is right for me" only actually qualify for one path once you filter by eligibility rather than preference. The comparison table matters for the minority of people who genuinely qualify for more than one, most commonly, someone who could get a work visa through a local employer but is also married to a Filipino citizen and has to decide which route to actually pursue.

## When you qualify for more than one

This is the actual decision point worth thinking through carefully, because it's rarely close to a coin flip once you look at what each path locks in long-term.

**Work visa vs. 13A, if you qualify for both:** A work visa ties your legal status to your job. If that job ends, your visa status is at risk, full stop, that's true even if the job change is your own choice, like leaving to start something independent. The 13A doesn't have that dependency. If you're eligible for 13A and also have work-visa-sponsoring employment, the 13A is almost always the more durable long-term structure, because your right to stay doesn't depend on staying employed by a specific company.

**SRRV vs. work visa, if you qualify for both:** SRRV gives you residency without work rights attached. You'd still need a separate work permit if you take local employment, though this doesn't affect remote/foreign income. A work visa gives you the opposite: work rights, but status dependent on that specific job. If your income is foreign-sourced and you're not trying to work for a Philippine company, SRRV is the cleaner structure. If a specific Philippine employer is the actual reason you're staying, the work visa is simpler to start with, and can be revisited later if the relationship with that employer becomes permanent (marriage) or if you build enough capital to shift to SRRV independence.

## What none of these three cover

If you're reading this and none of the three fit (no pension or spare $15K+, no local employer, not married), you're not out of options, you're just not the audience this particular comparison was built for. The Digital Nomad Visa, introduced in 2025, was built specifically for remote-income earners who don't fit any of these three boxes, and it's genuinely underused because most long-term-stay content online hasn't caught up to it yet. Full breakdown in the guide linked above.

## The part a comparison table can't do for you

Everything above assumes clean, textbook eligibility. Real situations are rarely that clean. Maybe you're 38 and will turn 40 before your current tourist extension runway runs out. Maybe you have a Philippine employer interested in sponsoring you, but you're also seeing someone you might marry within the year, and the sequencing of which visa you pursue first actually matters. Maybe your income is a mix of a US W-2 job winding down and a Philippine-based consulting contract starting up, and neither "work visa" nor "digital nomad" cleanly describes what you're doing.

That's not a gap in this article, it's the actual shape of most real situations, and it's exactly what a comparison table can't resolve because it was never built to handle timing, sequencing, or mixed circumstances.
