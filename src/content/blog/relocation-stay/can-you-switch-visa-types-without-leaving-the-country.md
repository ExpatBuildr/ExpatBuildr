Short answer: in the Philippines, usually yes. In Thailand, usually no. That single difference changes how you should plan your timeline in each country, and it's the kind of detail that doesn't show up until you're actually mid-process and it's suddenly a problem instead of a planning input.

Here's how conversion actually works in each country, and the specific traps that catch people who assume the rules are the same everywhere. See every guide in the [Relocation & Stay Strategy hub](/blog/relocation-stay).

## Philippines: in-country conversion is the norm, with one real trap

The Philippines generally lets you convert your tourist status to a long-term visa, SRRV or 13A, without leaving the country, provided your entry status is what's called "convertible." Most standard tourist entries qualify. You stay on your tourist visa, extending it as needed to remain in valid status, while you gather documents (police clearances, medical certificates, apostilles, pension proof or deposit remittance) and submit your SRRV or 13A application from inside the country.

**The trap: not every entry status is convertible, and the Philippine e-Visa is the one that most often catches people.** If you entered on an e-Visa, official guidance states it is explicitly **not valid for conversion to other visa types and not valid for extension** of your authorized stay. If your admission is marked "No Conversion," your only path forward is to leave and re-enter under a different, convertible status before you can apply for SRRV or 13A. This is a detail most general Philippines visa guides don't mention at all, and it's exactly the kind of thing that turns into a costly surprise if you find out about it after you've already started gathering SRRV paperwork.

**Practical sequencing that actually works:** arrive on a standard (convertible) tourist entry, start living in the Philippines, extend your tourist status every few months while gathering SRRV or 13A documents, then submit your application once your paperwork is complete. Processing typically runs 20-45 days depending on the source and completeness of your application. Your tourist status needs to stay valid and current the entire time you're waiting. Letting it lapse mid-process creates a separate problem on top of the one you're trying to solve.

## Thailand: leaving the country is usually required, and the DTV specifically requires it

Thailand draws a much harder line. Its immigration framework separates visa *extensions* (which you can do inside the country, within the same visa category) from visa *type changes* (converting to a genuinely different category), and only a narrow set of type changes are permitted without leaving.

**For the Destination Thailand Visa specifically, the answer is unambiguous: no.** Every official source on this is consistent: DTV applications must be submitted while you are physically outside Thailand. You can prepare your documents in advance while still in the country, but the application itself has to be filed from a Thai embassy or consulate abroad. This applies whether you're currently on a tourist visa, an ED (education) visa, or a Non-Immigrant B (work) visa. In every case, you cancel or let your current status lapse, leave, and apply for the DTV from outside.

Some other in-country conversions genuinely are possible: tourist visa or visa exemption status can sometimes convert to Non-Immigrant B (work), Non-Immigrant O (family/retirement), or Non-Immigrant ED (education) categories without leaving, typically requiring application at least 15 days before your current stay expires and roughly a 15-day processing window. But the DTV, which is the correct visa for most remote-income earners, sits outside that in-country pathway entirely.

**What this means practically:** if you're building toward the DTV and currently in Thailand on a tourist entry, plan an actual trip out of the country into your timeline. Popular options are nearby land borders (Laos, Cambodia, Malaysia) or regional hubs with experienced Thai consulates (Singapore, Kuala Lumpur). This isn't a formality you can skip: official guidance explicitly warns that any agent claiming they can process or "convert" your DTV application while you remain in Thailand is not offering something real, and relying on that claim puts your application at risk.

## Why this asymmetry matters for planning, not just paperwork

If you're weighing the Philippines against Thailand as your long-term base, which is a genuinely common comparison for people in this audience, this is one of the more concrete, practical differences between the two countries, and it rarely shows up in the lifestyle-and-cost-of-living comparisons that dominate most "Philippines vs Thailand" content. The Philippines lets you handle your entire transition to long-term status without interrupting your life there. Thailand, for its most relevant long-stay visa, requires you to build a trip abroad into your plan no matter what.
